ClaimPilot

AI Claims Intelligence Platform Business Plan & Problem Analysis

Introduction

This artifact comprises a comprehensive Problem Analysis and Business Plan for "ClaimPilot," an AI claims intelligence platform designed to address slow, manual insurance claims processing at Meridian Mutual Insurance. It was developed collaboratively during the AIML-505 R.3 Hack-a-Thon to demonstrate how generative AI can transform a traditional, high-volume claims workflow.

Artifact Description

  • Objective: To define the business problem of manual claims processing and propose a viable, AI-driven solution (ClaimPilot) complete with a structured implementation plan, success metrics, and ROI analysis.
  • Process: This was a group project created during a Hack-a-Thon. As the AI Product Manager, I led the development of the business plan, pitch, and success metrics, while collaborating closely with the Generative AI Engineer (solution design) and AI Data Engineer (data & governance) on the problem analysis.
  • Tools and Technologies Used: Large Language Models (Anthropic Claude on AWS Bedrock), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), Vector Database (pgvector), OCR pipelines, and enterprise data platforms.

Value Proposition and Audience

  • Personal Value Proposition: Demonstrates my competency as an AI Product Manager to bridge the gap between advanced technical AI capabilities and tangible business value, particularly in highly regulated domains like insurance.
  • Target Audience: Potential employers, hiring managers, and executives in insurtech or enterprise AI product management roles.

Artifact-Specific Value Proposition

  • Unique Value: This artifact showcases my strategic ability to define clear AI KPIs, estimate costs versus business benefits, and design human-in-the-loop workflows that effectively mitigate both regulatory and operational risks.
  • Relevance: It aligns perfectly with my personal value proposition by highlighting the strategic thinking, cross-functional collaboration, and product management skills crucial for leading enterprise AI initiatives.

Customization for Audience

  • Adaptations Made: The artifact was structured to emphasize ROI, high-level KPIs, and risk mitigation strategies (such as keeping the human adjuster in control), rather than just focusing on the technical architecture.
  • Relevance: These adaptations ensure the artifact is highly accessible and relevant to business-focused stakeholders who prioritize the practical, financial, and regulatory viability of enterprise AI solutions.

Reflection

  • Significance: I chose this artifact because it demonstrates an end-to-end cycle of AI product management—from deep problem identification and impact assessment to a structured, phased rollout plan. It highlights my ability to turn a technical concept into a compelling business case.
  • Lessons Learned: This project enhanced my understanding of the critical importance of human-in-the-loop design in regulated industries. It also reinforced how phasing AI implementations (e.g., starting with summarization before moving to fraud detection) is essential to manage risk, build trust, and prove value incrementally.

Feedback and Revisions

  • Feedback: Received input emphasizing the need to clearly define how the AI would interact with the adjusters' existing workflows without causing distrust or regulatory issues.
  • Revisions: Refined the solution overview to explicitly state that the AI (ClaimPilot) is embedded inside the existing workflow and that all outputs are advisory, ensuring the human adjuster remains the sole decision-maker.
